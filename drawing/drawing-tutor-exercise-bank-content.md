# Drawing tutor app — exercise bank content (all 16 skills)

This is the full drafted content for Stage 2 of the build (the exercise
bank). All 16 skills follow the same locked template: pass criteria (or
assessment notes for open-ended skills), drill variants, full exercise
variants with incidental-skill tags, and a reference-photo relevance note.
This content has already been reviewed and approved — build the bank
structure to hold this content as-is, rather than re-deriving it.

---

## Foundations

### 1. Line confidence *(checkable, no prerequisite)*

Pass criteria:
- Lines are single, confident strokes — not several short, overlapping
  corrective strokes
- Lines hold a reasonably straight/intended path over their full length
  (not wavering or arcing unintentionally)
- Line weight is reasonably consistent along the stroke (not trailing off
  or scratchy at the ends)

Drill variants:
1. 20 straight lines, freehand, mixing lengths and angles — one confident
   stroke per line
2. 20 straight lines drawn slowly, then 20 more drawn fast — same
   criteria, to separate control from confidence
3. 15 lines connecting two marked dots each (varying distance/angle) —
   tests aiming as well as stroke quality

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw a simple geometric pattern (e.g. a grid of triangles) freehand, no
   ruler — incidentally touches: Proportion by eye
2. Trace the outline of a real object in one continuous confident line, no
   lifting the pencil to correct — incidentally touches: Proportion by
   eye, Drawing from observation vs imagination

Reference photo: not needed for the drills. For full exercise variant 2,
a reference photo of the object being traced would help the AI judge
accuracy — optional.

---

### 2. Circle/curve control *(checkable, no prerequisite)*

Pass criteria:
- Circles/curves are single confident strokes
- Roundness is consistent (not lopsided or flat on one side)
- Closes cleanly near the start point without a big overlap or gap

Drill variants:
1. 20 circles, varying sizes, freehand
2. 20 curved lines of varying arc (shallow to tight), each in one stroke
3. 10 circles, then immediately 10 more inside/around them (nested) —
   tests control under closer spacing

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw a bowl of round fruit (any mix, any count) — incidentally touches:
   Proportion by eye
2. Draw a coiled rope or a plant with curling tendrils/vines —
   incidentally touches: Drawing from observation vs imagination

Reference photo: for full exercise variants, a reference photo of the
actual fruit/rope/plant would help — optional.

---

### 3. Ellipse consistency *(checkable, requires Circle/curve control)*

Pass criteria:
- Ellipse holds a consistent width-to-height ratio along its full curve
  (not egg-shaped unless intended)
- Both ends are symmetric
- No visible corners

Drill variants:
1. 20 ellipses, freehand, varying sizes and angles of rotation
2. A row of ellipses in a "table" — same size, rotating degree of tilt
   across the row
3. 10 ellipses, then draw a second ellipse tightly inside each (like a rim
   + base of a cup) — tests consistency at close spacing

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw 3–5 round objects viewed at an angle (mugs, jars, wheels — your
   choice of subject and count) — incidentally touches: Proportion by eye,
   Cylinders
2. Draw a table set from above/an angle with plates and cups —
   incidentally touches: Proportion by eye, Combining multiple objects

Reference photo: for full exercise variants, a reference photo of the
actual objects would help judge proportion accuracy — optional.

## Perspective

### 4. 1-point perspective *(checkable, requires Line confidence)*

Pass criteria:
- All converging lines actually meet at a single vanishing point on the
  horizon (not near-misses)
- Horizon line and vanishing point are clearly established before drawing
- Box faces read as flat planes, not warped

Drill variants:
1. 5 boxes in 1-point perspective, varying position relative to the
   vanishing point (above/below/either side of eye level)
2. Draw the vanishing point and horizon first, then 8 lines from scattered
   points all converging correctly into it
3. 3 boxes stacked or arranged in a row, all sharing the same vanishing
   point — tests consistency across multiple objects

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw a street, hallway, or train track receding into the distance
   (your choice of subject) — incidentally touches: Line confidence
2. Draw a simple building facade face-on with a receding side wall —
   incidentally touches: Boxes as volume

Reference photo: not needed for drills. For full exercise variants, a
reference photo of the actual street/building would help — optional.

---

### 5. 2-point perspective *(checkable, requires 1-point perspective)*

Pass criteria:
- Two vanishing points are both used consistently (lines actually converge
  to one or the other, not drifting)
- Vertical edges stay truly vertical
- Box proportions stay believable (not warped/stretched near the vanishing
  points)

Drill variants:
1. 5 boxes in 2-point perspective at varying distances from the vanishing
   points
2. One long horizon line with both vanishing points marked, then 10 lines
   drawn to test which point they should converge to
3. 3 boxes of different proportions (cube, tall, wide) using the same two
   vanishing points

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw a building corner from outside, at an angle (your choice of
   building complexity) — incidentally touches: Boxes as volume,
   Proportion by eye
2. Draw a piece of furniture (table, bookshelf, chest) at an angle —
   incidentally touches: Boxes as volume

Reference photo: for full exercise variants, a reference photo of the
actual building/furniture would help — optional.

---

### 6. Perspective in real spaces (interiors) *(checkable, requires 2-point perspective)*

Pass criteria:
- The room's walls/floor/ceiling agree on a single consistent perspective
  system (not mixing 1-point and 2-point inconsistently)
- Furniture within the room is sized/angled consistently with the room's
  own vanishing points

Drill variants:
1. Draw just the corner of a room (two walls + floor/ceiling meeting) — no
   furniture yet
2. Same corner, now add one simple box-shaped object (a box or crate)
   correctly sized within it
3. Draw the same corner from two different seated positions in the room —
   tests re-establishing vanishing points from scratch

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw a full corner of a real room with 2–3 pieces of furniture (your
   choice of room and how much furniture) — incidentally touches: Boxes as
   volume, Combining multiple objects, Proportion by eye
2. Draw a doorway or window seen at an angle within a wall —
   incidentally touches: Boxes as volume

Reference photo: not needed — this is drawn from the room directly in
front of the user.

## Proportion & measurement

### 7. Proportion by eye *(checkable, no prerequisite)*

Pass criteria:
- Relative sizes and placements of parts match the reference reasonably
  closely (e.g. a mug's height-to-width ratio, a face's eye spacing) —
  judged by comparison to the photo reference, not an abstract ideal

Drill variants:
1. Pick 3 simple objects, draw each in under 3 minutes focusing only on
   getting the proportions right — ignore all detail
2. Draw a single object twice: once estimating by eye only, once using the
   pencil-measuring technique (holding pencil at arm's length to compare
   lengths) — compare the two
3. Divide a rectangle you've drawn into equal thirds by eye, then check —
   repeat with quarters and fifths

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw a person's face from a photo, focusing only on proportion (feature
   placement), not likeness or shading — incidentally touches: Drawing
   from observation vs imagination
2. Draw a full-body figure from a photo focusing only on proportion
   (limb/torso ratios) — incidentally touches: Drawing from observation vs
   imagination

Reference photo: YES, needed for accurate assessment — a reference photo
of the object/face/figure being drawn is important here, since the pass
criteria explicitly compares the drawing to the reference.

---

### 8. Measuring angles and lengths *(checkable, requires Proportion by eye)*

Pass criteria:
- Angles in the drawing are reasonably close to the reference's actual
  angles (not defaulted to vertical/horizontal out of habit)
- Relative lengths between parts are consistent with the reference

Drill variants:
1. Draw 10 lines at angles you first estimate by eye, then check with a
   protractor/ruler afterwards — track how close you got
2. Pick an object with a clear diagonal or tilt (a tilted book, a leaning
   ladder) and draw just that one angle correctly
3. Draw a simple object using only straight measuring lines first (no
   curves) to block in proportions before any detail

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw an object with several non-90-degree angles (an open laptop, a
   step ladder, a wedge of cheese — your choice) — incidentally touches:
   Proportion by eye
2. Draw a chair or stool from an angle where the legs splay at different
   angles — incidentally touches: Proportion by eye, Boxes as volume

Reference photo: YES, needed — same reasoning as Proportion by eye, since
angle/length accuracy is judged against the actual reference.

## Form

### 9. Cylinders *(checkable, requires Circle/curve control)*

Pass criteria:
- The two end ellipses of the cylinder are consistent with each other in
  proportion
- The cylinder's sides are parallel (not tapering unintentionally)
- Basic shading (if included) reads as consistent with a single light
  direction

Drill variants:
1. 3 cylinders from different angles (upright, tilted, lying down), line
   only, no shading
2. Same 3 cylinders, now add simple shading to suggest roundness
3. Draw one cylinder, then a second one that's clearly narrower/wider —
   tests proportion consistency across variants

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw a mug, glass, or can from observation (your choice) —
   incidentally touches: Ellipse consistency, Rendering form with shading
2. Draw a stack of 2–3 cylindrical objects (cans, rolls, tubes) —
   incidentally touches: Combining multiple objects, Proportion by eye

Reference photo: for full exercise variants, a reference photo of the
actual object(s) would help — optional.

---

### 10. Spheres *(checkable, requires Circle/curve control)*

Pass criteria:
- The sphere reads as fully round (shading wraps convincingly, not flat or
  "circle with shadow")
- Light source direction is consistent across the sphere's surface

Drill variants:
1. 3 spheres, line only
2. Same 3 spheres, add shading using at least 4 distinct values to suggest
   roundness
3. Draw one sphere lit from three different directions (three separate
   spheres, same size) — tests deliberate control of light placement

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw a round fruit (orange, apple) with shading from observation —
   incidentally touches: Rendering form with shading, Light source
   consistency
2. Draw 2–3 spheres of different sizes together with a single consistent
   light source — incidentally touches: Combining multiple objects, Light
   source consistency

Reference photo: for full exercise variants, a reference photo of the
actual fruit/objects would help — optional.

---

### 11. Boxes as volume *(checkable, requires 1-point perspective)*

Pass criteria:
- The box reads as a solid volume, not a flat outline (achieved through
  correct perspective lines and/or shading)
- All visible faces are proportionally consistent with each other

Drill variants:
1. 5 boxes freehand in 1-point perspective, line only
2. Same 5 boxes, add flat shading per face (one tone per visible face) to
   reinforce volume
3. Draw one box, then a second box resting on top at a slight rotation —
   tests maintaining volume across two related forms

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw a stack of books or boxes (your choice of count/arrangement) —
   incidentally touches: Combining multiple objects, Proportion by eye
2. Draw a piece of furniture built from box-like forms (a drawer unit, a
   shelf) — incidentally touches: 2-point perspective

Reference photo: for full exercise variants, a reference photo of the
actual object(s) would help — optional.

## Value & shading

### 12. Value scale control *(checkable, no prerequisite)*

Pass criteria:
- The value scale shows genuinely distinct, evenly-spaced steps from white
  to black (not clustered in the middle, not skipping straight from light
  to dark)
- Pressure/technique is controlled, not scratchy or uneven within a single
  step

Drill variants:
1. A 6-step value scale strip using only pencil pressure, no
   cross-hatching
2. Same 6-step scale, this time using a different technique
   (cross-hatching or circular scribbling) — compare control between
   techniques
3. A 10-step scale (finer gradation) — tests control at the harder, more
   granular end

Full exercise variants: NONE — this is intentionally a pure isolated drill
skill. A value scale isn't naturally something drawn "as a scene," so no
full exercise variant is defined for this skill.

Reference photo: not applicable.

---

### 13. Rendering form with shading *(open-ended, requires Value scale control)*

Assessment notes (no pass/fail — periodic "are you happy with this?"
check-ins instead, once feedback is consistently clear of issues):
- Does the shading follow the form's actual surface (not just "dark
  side/light side" flatly applied)?
- Are at least 3–4 distinct values used rather than 2?
- Do edges/transitions look intentional rather than uniformly smudged?

Drill variants:
1. Shade one sphere using at least 4 values
2. Shade one cylinder and one box side-by-side, same light source — tests
   applying the same light logic across different forms
3. Shade the same simple object twice, using dramatically different light
   harshness (soft diffuse light vs harsh direct light)

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw and shade a single still-life object of your choice from
   observation — incidentally touches: whichever form skill matches the
   object, Light source consistency
2. Draw and shade a simple draped cloth or crumpled paper —
   incidentally touches: Drawing from observation vs imagination

Reference photo: for full exercise variants, a reference photo of the
actual object/cloth would help — optional.

---

### 14. Light source consistency *(open-ended, requires Rendering form with shading)*

Assessment notes:
- Is there one clear, identifiable light direction across the whole
  drawing (not each object shaded as if lit separately)?
- Do cast shadows (if included) agree with that same light direction?

Drill variants:
1. Draw 2 simple forms (a sphere and a box) lit from the same
   clearly-stated direction
2. Same 2 forms, now add a cast shadow for each consistent with that light
   direction
3. Draw one object, shade it, then redraw it shaded from a different light
   direction — compare

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw a small still life (2–3 objects, your choice) with one light
   source and consistent cast shadows — incidentally touches: Combining
   multiple objects, Rendering form with shading
2. Draw a room corner or desk setup with a single light source affecting
   everything in view — incidentally touches: Perspective in real spaces,
   Combining multiple objects

Reference photo: for full exercise variants, a reference photo of the
actual setup would help — optional.

## Composition

### 15. Combining multiple objects *(open-ended, requires Proportion by eye AND one form skill)*

Assessment notes:
- Do objects sit in believable spatial relationship to each other
  (correct relative scale, overlap, and placement on a shared ground)?
- Does the arrangement read as one coherent scene rather than separate
  objects pasted together?

Note: this skill is inherently about combination, so drills and full
exercises overlap more than other skills — drills below are deliberately
kept simpler/smaller-scale versions of the same idea rather than isolated
technique reps.

Drill variants:
1. Draw 2 objects of clearly different sizes next to each other, focusing
   only on getting their relative scale right
2. Draw 2 objects where one partially overlaps/occludes the other
3. Draw 3 objects in a loose row at different distances from you
   (near/mid/far) — tests scale-with-distance

Full exercise variants:
1. Set up 2–3 objects together (your choice — mug, book, fruit, etc.) and
   draw the whole group — incidentally touches: Proportion by eye,
   relevant form skills
2. Draw a corner of a real desk or shelf as-is, with whatever's actually
   sitting there — incidentally touches: Drawing from observation vs
   imagination, Proportion by eye

Reference photo: not needed — this is drawn from a real arrangement
directly in front of the user.

---

### 16. Drawing from observation vs imagination *(open-ended, no prerequisite)*

Assessment notes:
- For observation tasks: does the drawing genuinely match specifics of the
  real reference (not generic/idealised)?
- For imagination tasks: is the result internally consistent (proportions,
  perspective, light agree with each other even though invented)?

Drill variants:
1. Draw a real object in front of you, then draw the same type of object
   entirely from memory/imagination — compare the two side by side
2. Draw a real object, covering it after 30 seconds and finishing from
   memory
3. Invent a simple object (a made-up creature, tool, or container) and
   draw it from imagination, applying perspective/form rules deliberately

Full exercise variants:
1. Draw a real scene in front of you (any subject/setting you choose) —
   incidentally touches: Proportion by eye, Combining multiple objects
2. Invent and draw a short scene from imagination (a room, a creature in a
   setting) — incidentally touches: Combining multiple objects,
   Perspective in real spaces

Reference photo: for observation-based variants, a reference photo of the
real object/scene may help the AI compare — optional. Not applicable for
imagination-based variants.
